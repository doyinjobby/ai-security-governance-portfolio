
# AI Governance Roles and Accountability (RACI) Matrix

**Author:** Dr. Olajumoke Bello, PhD  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Classification:** Public Portfolio Sample  
**Status:** Illustrative AI Governance and Accountability Deliverable

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes an illustrative Roles and Accountability Matrix for governing AI systems throughout their lifecycle.

The objective is to define clear ownership, decision-making authority, operational responsibilities, consultation requirements and governance reporting arrangements.

The model supports risk-based AI governance aligned with the principles of NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and established cybersecurity governance practices.

**Guiding Principle: Protect to Enable**

Effective AI governance requires clear accountability, appropriate human oversight and evidence-based decisions.

---

## 2. Scope and Applicability

This accountability model may apply to:

- Internally developed AI systems
- Third-party AI applications
- Generative AI and large language model solutions
- AI-enabled business processes
- AI systems processing personal or sensitive information
- AI systems integrated with enterprise applications
- AI systems supporting consequential decisions

Responsibilities should be adapted to organisational structure, AI system criticality, risk appetite and applicable legal obligations.

---

## 3. RACI Definitions

**R — Responsible**

The role performing or coordinating the activity and producing the required deliverable.

**A — Accountable**

The role ultimately answerable for the activity's outcome and authorised to approve its completion.

**C — Consulted**

Stakeholders providing specialist advice or input before a decision or activity is completed.

**I — Informed**

Stakeholders receiving relevant decisions, results, changes or status updates.

### RACI Governance Rules

- Each defined activity should have one clearly identified accountable role.
- Multiple roles may be responsible or consulted.
- Accountability must be supported by appropriate decision-making authority.
- Responsibilities should be documented and communicated.
- Material risks and exceptions must follow defined escalation procedures.
- RACI assignments do not replace statutory, regulatory or professional responsibilities.
- Segregation of duties and independent assurance should be maintained where appropriate.

## 4. AI Governance Roles

### 4.1 Executive Leadership

- Approves organisational AI governance strategy.
- Establishes AI risk appetite and tolerance.
- Provides resources and strategic oversight.
- Receives reporting on material AI risks and exceptions.
- Makes decisions reserved for executive authority.

### 4.2 AI Governance or Risk Committee

- Oversees the AI governance programme.
- Reviews significant AI risks and assurance findings.
- Coordinates cross-functional governance decisions.
- Reviews policy exceptions and risk escalations.
- Oversees deployment governance and periodic reassessment.
- Escalates matters beyond delegated authority.

### 4.3 AI System Owner

- Maintains accountability for the assigned AI system.
- Documents intended use and business justification.
- Maintains system inventory and risk information.
- Coordinates risk assessments and remediation.
- Ensures operational monitoring and change reporting.
- Confirms that required governance evidence is available.

### 4.4 Information Security

- Evaluates AI cybersecurity risks.
- Defines security requirements and safeguards.
- Coordinates AI security testing and control validation.
- Reviews vulnerabilities and security findings.
- Supports incident response and security monitoring.
- Provides security assurance recommendations.

### 4.5 Privacy, Legal and Compliance

- Reviews privacy and regulatory obligations.
- Advises on data protection and lawful processing.
- Evaluates contractual and compliance requirements.
- Supports impact assessments and governance decisions.
- Advises on legal restrictions and reporting obligations.

### 4.6 Engineering and Operations

- Implements technical security controls.
- Maintains system configuration and integrations.
- Supports AI testing and remediation.
- Maintains logging, monitoring and operational safeguards.
- Implements approved changes and deployment restrictions.
- Supports technical incident investigation.

### 4.7 Third-Party Risk Management

- Coordinates AI supplier due diligence.
- Reviews supplier security and governance evidence.
- Assesses material vendor and fourth-party dependencies.
- Tracks supplier assurance findings and remediation.
- Monitors significant supplier changes.
- Supports procurement and contractual risk decisions.

## 5. AI Governance RACI Assignments

The following assignments represent an illustrative operating model. Organisations should formally approve role assignments and delegated authorities before operational use.

### Activity 1 — Establish AI Governance Strategy and Risk Appetite

- **Accountable (A):** Executive Leadership
- **Responsible (R):** AI Governance or Risk Committee
- **Consulted (C):** Information Security; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; AI System Owner
- **Informed (I):** Engineering and Operations; Third-Party Risk Management

**Evidence:** Approved AI Governance Strategy, Risk Appetite Statement and Governance Committee Terms of Reference.

### Activity 2 — Maintain AI System Inventory

- **Accountable (A):** AI System Owner
- **Responsible (R):** AI System Owner
- **Consulted (C):** Engineering and Operations; Information Security; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee; Privacy, Legal and Compliance

**Evidence:** AI System Inventory, System Profile and Ownership Records.

### Activity 3 — Assess AI Use Cases and Potential Impacts

- **Accountable (A):** AI System Owner
- **Responsible (R):** AI System Owner
- **Consulted (C):** Information Security; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Engineering and Operations; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee

**Evidence:** AI Use Case Assessment, Stakeholder Analysis and AI Impact Assessment.

### Activity 4 — Conduct AI Cybersecurity Risk Assessment

- **Accountable (A):** AI System Owner
- **Responsible (R):** Information Security
- **Consulted (C):** Engineering and Operations; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee

**Evidence:** AI Risk Assessment, Risk Register and Control Evaluation Records.

### Activity 5 — Conduct AI Security Testing and Validation

- **Accountable (A):** Information Security
- **Responsible (R):** Information Security; Engineering and Operations
- **Consulted (C):** AI System Owner; Privacy, Legal and Compliance
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee

**Evidence:** AI Security Test Plan, Test Results, Findings Register and Retest Evidence.

### Activity 6 — Conduct Third-Party AI Supplier Assurance

- **Accountable (A):** AI System Owner
- **Responsible (R):** Third-Party Risk Management
- **Consulted (C):** Information Security; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Engineering and Operations
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee

**Evidence:** Supplier Due Diligence Assessment, Assurance Evidence Checklist, Supplier Risk Assessment and Remediation Tracker.

### Activity 7 — Implement AI Risk Treatment and Remediation

- **Accountable (A):** AI System Owner
- **Responsible (R):** Engineering and Operations
- **Consulted (C):** Information Security; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee

**Evidence:** AI Risk Treatment Plan, Remediation Tracker, Control Validation Results and Residual Risk Assessment.

### Activity 8 — Approve Residual AI Risk Acceptance

- **Accountable (A):** AI Governance or Risk Committee
- **Responsible (R):** AI System Owner
- **Consulted (C):** Information Security; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** Engineering and Operations; Executive Leadership

**Evidence:** Residual Risk Assessment, Risk Acceptance Record and Approval Decision.

**Governance condition:** The committee may approve only risks within its formally delegated authority. Risks exceeding that authority must be referred to Executive Leadership or another designated risk acceptance authority.

### Activity 9 — Authorise AI System Deployment

- **Accountable (A):** AI Governance or Risk Committee
- **Responsible (R):** AI System Owner
- **Consulted (C):** Information Security; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Engineering and Operations; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** Executive Leadership

**Evidence:** Deployment Readiness Assessment, Security Assurance Results, Outstanding Risk Record and Deployment Approval.

**Governance condition:** Deployment authorisation requires confirmation that applicable mandatory controls, approvals and risk acceptance conditions have been satisfied.

### Activity 10 — Maintain Continuous AI Monitoring

- **Accountable (A):** AI System Owner
- **Responsible (R):** Engineering and Operations; Information Security
- **Consulted (C):** Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee

**Evidence:** AI Monitoring Plan, Monitoring Reports, Security Findings and Operational Risk Indicators.

### Activity 11 — Manage AI Security Incidents

- **Accountable (A):** Information Security
- **Responsible (R):** Information Security; Engineering and Operations
- **Consulted (C):** AI System Owner; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee; Executive Leadership, according to incident severity

**Evidence:** Incident Record, Investigation Findings, Containment Actions, Recovery Evidence and Lessons Learned.

**Governance condition:** Incident command, breach notification and crisis management responsibilities must follow the organisation's approved incident response and legal reporting procedures.

### Activity 12 — Reassess AI Systems Following Material Changes

- **Accountable (A):** AI System Owner
- **Responsible (R):** AI System Owner; Information Security
- **Consulted (C):** Engineering and Operations; Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** AI Governance or Risk Committee

**Evidence:** Change Record, Updated Risk Assessment, Control Validation Results and Reassessment Decision.

**Governance condition:** Where a change invalidates the original deployment approval, renewed authorisation must be obtained from the designated approval authority before continued operation under the changed conditions.

### Activity 13 — Produce AI Governance and Assurance Reports

- **Accountable (A):** AI Governance or Risk Committee
- **Responsible (R):** AI System Owner; Information Security
- **Consulted (C):** Privacy, Legal and Compliance; Engineering and Operations; Third-Party Risk Management
- **Informed (I):** Executive Leadership

**Evidence:** Governance Dashboard, Risk Reports, Assurance Findings, Remediation Status and Committee Minutes.

## 6. Risk Escalation and Decision Authority

AI risks should be escalated according to defined organisational thresholds.

### Operational-Level Risks

Risks within approved tolerance may be managed by the AI System Owner, subject to established policies and delegated authority.

### Material or High Risks

Material risks should be escalated to the AI Governance or Risk Committee for review, treatment direction and approval decisions within its delegated authority.

### Critical or Unacceptable Risks

Risks exceeding delegated tolerance should be escalated to Executive Leadership or the designated enterprise risk authority.

Deployment or continued operation may require restrictions, additional safeguards, suspension or renewed authorisation.

### Regulatory and Legal Matters

Privacy, Legal and Compliance must be consulted where relevant legal, regulatory, contractual or notification obligations arise.

## 7. Human Oversight and Accountability

Human oversight should be proportionate to the potential consequences of AI system outputs and actions.

The AI System Owner should ensure that:

- Appropriate human review points are defined.
- Decision-making authority is documented.
- High-impact AI outputs receive suitable review.
- Human intervention and override mechanisms are available where required.
- Escalation routes are understood.
- Decisions and approvals are recorded.
- Automated actions remain within authorised boundaries.

Human oversight should be tested and reviewed for effectiveness, rather than assumed to be adequate because a human is nominally involved.

---

## 8. Continuous Governance and Reassessment

AI governance accountability must remain effective after initial approval.

### Reassessment Triggers

- Model updates or replacements
- Changes to intended use
- New data sources
- New AI vendors or model providers
- New external integrations or tool permissions
- Material architecture changes
- Significant AI security incidents
- New attack techniques or vulnerabilities
- Material regulatory changes
- Evidence of control failure or unacceptable outcomes

### Reassessment Process

1. Identify and record the change or trigger.
2. Determine whether the existing risk assessment remains valid.
3. Reassess affected risks and controls.
4. Perform additional testing or supplier assurance where necessary.
5. Update the risk register and governance evidence.
6. Escalate material changes and unacceptable residual risks.
7. Obtain renewed authorisation where required.
8. Continue monitoring and validating controls.

Continuous monitoring provides visibility.

Continuous validation provides evidence.

Governance determines whether the system remains acceptable for operation.

## 10. Governance Review and Maintenance

This RACI Matrix should be reviewed:

- At defined governance review intervals
- Following significant organisational restructuring
- When AI ownership or approval authority changes
- Following material incidents or governance failures
- When new AI technologies or dependencies are introduced
- Following material changes to applicable legal or regulatory obligations

Changes to accountability assignments should be approved, documented and communicated.

Implementation of this accountability model should support:

- Clear ownership of AI systems and associated risks
- Defined approval and escalation authority
- Consistent AI security and governance responsibilities
- Improved coordination across business and technical functions
- Stronger supplier assurance and risk management
- Evidence-based deployment decisions

## 12. Conclusion

AI governance requires more than policies and technical controls.

It requires identifiable owners, documented responsibilities, authorised decision-makers and continuous assurance.

A clearly defined RACI model helps organisations translate AI governance principles into operational accountability throughout the AI system lifecycle.

**Protect to Enable — Clear Accountability, Responsible AI Adoption and Continuous Assurance.**
